Muay Thai training holiday.

By Tim & Paemi · 9 min read · Updated 2026-08-02 · How we rank →

Compare a residential week, training-only passes and contact-first camps using dated operator prices, exact inclusions and a reversible first-booking plan.

Updated 2026-08-02 · Pattaya · Compare camps · Plan trip

A Pattaya Muay Thai training holiday is a sequence of purchases: training, room, meals, local transport, equipment and change terms. Some operators combine those items; others sell training only. The useful comparison is therefore not “best camp” in the abstract, but which exact transaction fits the intended week, coaching level and tolerance for commitment.

If you only read one thing: buy the shortest product that answers your biggest uncertainty, then extend only after the coach, class, room and route have proved suitable. A published one-week residential package, a weekly class pass and one drop-in session are different products. Get the complete inclusion list and total in writing before paying.

Start with the accommodation comparison, the beginner guide and the venue comparison tool. Each linked venue record carries its verification date and unresolved questions.

The real choice: bundled camp or independent trip

A bundled residential camp reduces coordination. The same operator can quote the room and training, and may specify meals, laundry or transfers. That convenience also concentrates risk: if the room, class level or location is wrong, both training and accommodation are tied to one booking.

An independent trip separates the decisions. Book a short room near a gym, test the actual class, then extend. This can make switching easier, but the traveller must confirm every commute, meal and equipment cost. The approach works best when the gym publishes a weekly product or accepts a named first session.

Neither route is universally cheaper. Compare the same bundle. A training-only month cannot be placed beside a residential week without adding room, meals, taxes and local travel. Likewise, a contact-first camp is not automatically expensive; it simply does not provide a dependable public number.

How to choose the first commitment

One session

A single session is the most reversible test. It can answer whether the entrance is easy to find, the session is suitable for the stated level, coaching communication works and the contact level is acceptable. It does not show how the same gym feels through a full training week or whether accommodation is suitable.

Use a named published product where possible. Battle & Conquer publishes a THB 500 group session. Fairtex publishes THB 800 for Muay Thai. Sityodtong publishes THB 400 for individual pad work. Those labels are not interchangeable; confirm duration, group format, equipment and whether warm-up or open-gym time is included.

One week

A weekly product suits a short holiday or a trial before extending. Battle & Conquer publishes THB 2,500 for its group-training package. ISS publishes THB 4,000 for boxing and Muay Thai classes. Kombat’s THB 20,900 standard week is residential and carries the operator’s stated VAT and card-fee conditions. The price gap reflects a different purchase, not evidence that one gym is universally better value.

One month

A month creates exposure to schedule changes, minor injury, room dissatisfaction and a route that looks easy on a map but is inconvenient in daily use. Fairtex, Battle & Conquer and ISS publish monthly training products, while Sityodtong and Sitpholek require more direct clarification. Ask whether the month starts at purchase, first attendance or a fixed calendar date, and whether pauses or transfers are allowed.

Residential options: verify the room separately

Kombat Group publishes residential programme tables and charges that vary by room and package. Its standard one-week product gives a numeric anchor, but a traveller still needs the exact accommodation category, training discipline, meal schedule, transfer terms, taxes and card fee.

Fairtex publishes training prices and separate accommodation-and-training packages. Do not assume the training-only month includes a hotel room, pool access or meals. Ask for the named package page, room category, single or shared occupancy, check-in rules and every included facility.

Rage Fight Academy documents on-site rooms, a pool and several combat and fitness disciplines, but no stable public residential tariff was available in the checked pages. Its breadth can suit a group whose members want different training, yet it also makes the written quote more important: identify which disciplines, room services and sessions are included.

Sityodtong presents a four-week camp route with housing, but the checked public pages did not expose a complete current residential price. Sitpholek’s old website says rooms were unavailable at that time and lists nearby historic figures; those statements are not a current accommodation offer.

Build the schedule from operator times, not a generic fight-camp day

There is no one Pattaya timetable. Fairtex lists centre hours from 07:30 to 20:00 Monday through Saturday, with programme times varying. Battle & Conquer publishes group Muay Thai at 08:00-10:00 and 16:00-18:00 daily. ISS publishes boxing and Muay Thai classes Monday through Saturday from 14:00 to 15:30. Sityodtong publishes 08:00-10:00 and 15:00-17:30 Monday through Saturday.

Sitpholek illustrates why Maps hours cannot be copied into a holiday plan. Maps lists 08:00-18:00 daily, while the old operator page describes Sunday as rest. Contact the gym for the exact current class. Rage lists business hours but not a dependable class-by-class timetable in its checked public pages.

Choose accommodation only after the intended class window is confirmed. A room that appears near “Pattaya” can still be on the wrong side of the training location. Use exact pins, not a broad area label or an invented travel time. The East Pattaya page, Central Pattaya page and Pratamnak page provide orientation, but the operator must confirm the entrance.

A realistic first-week structure

Do not promise yourself two daily sessions before experiencing the heat, class load and travel. A conservative first week leaves room to learn the product:

  • Arrival day: locate the gym, confirm the booking and collect any rental equipment; do not infer that a late arrival moves a reserved session.
  • First training day: attend the named product and state experience, contact boundaries and equipment needs.
  • Days two and three: repeat only if recovery and the coach fit are acceptable; confirm whether the afternoon format differs from morning.
  • Midweek: review room, route, meal and laundry logistics before extending.
  • Final booked day: request the next product and full amount in writing instead of assuming the same per-day rate scales to a week or month.

This is a planning framework, not medical or coaching advice. Anyone with a health concern should use an appropriately qualified professional. The Muay Thai safety guide focuses on session choice, contact and questions to ask without promising a risk-free class.

The written quote that prevents most disputes

Ask the operator to name the product, dates and total, then answer:

  1. How many group and private sessions are included, and at what times?
  2. Are pad work, clinch, sparring, open gym or recovery areas included or optional?
  3. Which room, occupancy, bathroom, housekeeping and check-in terms apply?
  4. Which meals, laundry, airport transfer or local transport are included?
  5. Are VAT, card fees, registration, equipment, deposit or trainer tips additional?
  6. What happens if a session, arrival or part of the stay changes?

Save the answer and date. A current written quote is more reliable than a precise old blog figure. For a venue with blank pricing, contact-first is the honest outcome; it is not a closure signal.

Entry rules and long stays

This guide does not tell a traveller which Thai immigration permission applies. Entry and extension rules depend on passport and current official policy. Check the appropriate Thai government or embassy source for the intended dates. A gym sales page or old forum post should not be treated as legal advice.

For a multi-month plan, separate the immigration question from the training decision. First establish that the course and operator fit; then verify the current lawful route from an authoritative source. The directory’s training and visa guide is a question framework, not a substitute for official rules.

Make the call

Choose Kombat when a current residential package and a single operator relationship matter more than flexibility. Choose a published week at Battle & Conquer or ISS when accommodation should stay separate. Use Fairtex when its specific training or package menu fits the requested product. Use Sityodtong for the named pad-work entry or contact it for a longer route. Contact Sitpholek and Rage when their verified facilities or locations fit, while accepting that the decisive price must come from a current reply.

Training-holiday questions

Should a beginner book one month of Muay Thai before arriving?

A month can be appropriate, but it is a large commitment to one class, coach, room and route. A shorter named product can test those variables first. If booking a month, obtain session frequency, start date, pauses, equipment, accommodation and change terms in writing.

Which current Pattaya option has a published residential week?

Kombat Group publishes a one-week standard Muay Thai or western-boxing residential product at THB 20,900, checked 25 July 2026. The operator says 7% VAT is excluded and card payment adds 3%. Compare the exact room and inclusions, not only the headline.

What does a blank training price mean?

It means no dependable current public tariff was verified. It does not mean the gym is closed, free or unwilling to accept visitors. Contact the exact operator for the named session, dates, inclusions, total, payment and change terms.
