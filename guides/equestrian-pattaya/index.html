Equestrian & polo.

By TimPaemi · 10 min read · Updated 2026-07-27 · How we rank →

Pattaya’s two equestrian records serve different decisions. Horseshoe Point publishes ordinary riding-school products and has current 2026 competition use; Thai Polo documents polo, eventing and specialist facilities. Neither publishes a simple current visitor tariff, so the first booking message matters.

Pattaya · 215 venues hand-checked · Compare gyms

If you only read one thing: start with Horseshoe Point for an ordinary riding enquiry and Thai Polo & Equestrian Club for polo, eventing or competition-specific access. That is a fit decision, not a ranking. Both venues are active in Pong, east of the beach districts, and both require direct confirmation because neither publishes a complete current public riding tariff.

The equestrian directory contains these two records. It does not support a city-wide “horse-riding price” because the products are different: a private lesson, group lesson, pony ride, trail ride, polo clinic, competition entry and spectator event are not substitutes. Current operator pages establish the facilities and contact routes; 2026 federation schedules establish recent competition use. Neither source type proves that an unbooked visitor can arrive and ride.

The strongest differentiator is transaction clarity. Horseshoe Point’s riding-school page names formats that a general visitor can ask for. Thai Polo’s site documents a much larger specialist complex and several polo products, but the access process is more likely to depend on the rider, horse, event and date. A large estate or prestigious facility does not by itself make a casual booking easier.

Horseshoe Point for the ordinary riding enquiry

Horseshoe Point is at 100 Moo 9 in Pong. Its current riding-school page lists private lessons, group lessons, trail rides and pony rides. Those four labels are useful because they let a reader ask for the right product. A lesson is instruction; a trail ride is a route-based experience; a pony ride may have different age, height and supervision rules. Do not ask only for “horse riding” and assume the answer covers all four.

The site’s current contact page publishes +66 38 735 050, while the live Maps identity lists +66 63 994 7165. The venue record retains both rather than choosing one as silently more authoritative. Use the operator number first for a riding enquiry and keep the live-listing number as a second route if the first is unanswered. Ask staff to send the exact meeting point because the resort and equestrian facilities occupy a broader property than a single street-front shop.

Current operation is supported by more than an old resort page. A federation schedule approved for 13–15 March 2026 identifies the showground as Chonburi–HSP, gives a Horseshoe Point email contact and documents an outdoor CSI1* competition. The schedule specifies a 55-by-75-metre sand competition arena and a 20-by-63-metre sand practice arena. Those figures describe the named competition setup. They do not establish the arena used for a tourist lesson or the condition of a surface on another date.

No current public lesson fee, trail-ride price, pony-ride price, weekly timetable or non-resident booking policy was published during the latest check. Horseshoe Point is therefore the more straightforward first enquiry, not a guaranteed walk-in. Send the rider’s age, experience, approximate height and weight, preferred date and desired format. Ask whether a beginner assessment is required and whether an event blocks normal riding access.

Thai Polo for specialist polo and equestrian use

Thai Polo & Equestrian Club publishes a much broader competition and club footprint. The operator states that the property covers more than 2,000 rai, approximately 250 hectares. Its facilities page lists three full-sized polo fields, two practice fields, cross-country and eventing courses, stables and an equine clinic. The about page also documents more than 250 stables and describes horses available for members and guests.

Those numbers show scale, not visitor eligibility. The live Maps listing gives 08:00–17:00 Tuesday to Sunday and Monday closed, but opening hours do not mean a polo lesson, trail ride, clubhouse visit or competition is available throughout that window. The operator’s contact page separates polo and equestrian enquiries and publishes a named Thai equestrian contact. Use the correct channel and state the intended activity.

The club’s polo page describes a progression from lessons into beginner chukkas, club chukkas and a weekend polo clinic. It also describes a trail ride across the property. A beginner chukka is not necessarily a first-ever riding lesson: the operator says it follows lessons and introduces players to a small game format. A weekend clinic combines morning lesson time with afternoon chukkas. Ask what prior riding competence is required before treating either as a beginner product.

The competition evidence is current. The official 2025/26 material lists Thai Polo Open and Queen’s Cup Pink Polo dates, and an FEI-approved schedule documents competition at Thai Polo Club House 1 in February 2026. Event evidence helps a spectator or competitor distinguish an active venue from a dormant listing. It still does not create a standing public calendar after those dates. Ask whether the intended event is open to spectators, whether registration is needed and which gate is in use.

Price comparison stops where the evidence stops

Neither venue publishes a current ordinary visitor tariff that can support a numeric side-by-side table. Horseshoe Point does not show lesson, pony-ride or trail-ride rates. Thai Polo does not show lesson, chukka, clinic, trail-ride, membership or spectator prices. Blank price fields mean unknown, not free and not necessarily expensive.

Competition documents may contain entry fees for registered athletes and horses. Those are event-administration products governed by federation rules; they are not evidence of the cost of a lesson or casual ride. Likewise, a hotel room or resort package should not be treated as including riding unless the operator names that inclusion for the exact booking.

Request a written quote with the activity name, session duration, number of riders, instruction level, horse provision, helmet and boot arrangements, taxes, deposit, cancellation rule and weather policy. For polo, add horse hire, tack, groom support, chukka count and the rider standard required. A complete expensive quote can be easier to compare than a low headline number with most essentials omitted.

Fit by rider and trip type

First-time adult or child: Horseshoe Point is the clearer first contact because its site explicitly names private lessons, group lessons and pony rides. That does not guarantee acceptance. Confirm age, size, supervision and instructor availability before placing transport around the booking.

Existing rider: choose by discipline. Ask Horseshoe about the relevant lesson format and arena; ask Thai Polo about polo, eventing, cross-country or competition-specific use. Send an honest experience summary. “Experienced” can mean very different things in trail riding, dressage, show jumping and polo.

Polo learner or player: Thai Polo is the purpose-built enquiry. Name whether you need an introductory lesson, beginner chukkas, club chukkas or a weekend clinic. Confirm whether the programme supplies the horse and equipment and whether the stated contact still handles the selected product.

Spectator: choose an exact dated event rather than visiting during general Maps hours. Thai Polo’s current-season pages and the federation schedules provide event evidence; Horseshoe’s March 2026 schedule documents recent show-jumping use. After an event date passes, it is evidence of operation, not a future ticket.

Family with mixed interests: the riding decision should still be made rider by rider. A child’s pony product, an adult lesson and a spectator programme can have different eligibility and timings. The family-friendly sport guide compares other current child products without implying that a family label overrides an operator’s age or supervision rule.

Location and return planning

Both records are in Pong within the wider East Pattaya belt, but their addresses and gates are different. Horseshoe Point is at 100 Moo 9. Thai Polo is at 111 Polo Drive, Moo 9. A shared district does not prove the properties are adjacent, share an entrance or use the same route around an event.

The Thai Polo contact page publishes driving directions via Route 36 and Bira Racing Circuit, plus an alternative approach through Soi Siam Country Club and Mabprachan Reservoir. Those directions are more useful than a generic “near Pattaya” label, but a current map pin and staff confirmation should control. The East Pattaya area page explains why this inland index is not one compact walkable neighbourhood.

Arrange the return as carefully as the outward trip. Neither operator publishes a universal public-transport route, ride-hailing pickup promise, fixed transfer fare or journey time from Beach Road. An event finish, child’s lesson or trail ride may end away from the initial enquiry desk. Ask for the gate name, staff contact and pickup point in the booking reply.

Booking and safety questions

Send one structured message instead of several vague questions. Include rider count, each rider’s age and experience, desired discipline, date, preferred time and language requirement. Ask whether the session is private or shared, how long it lasts, what happens in rain, and whether the operator can accommodate the rider’s size and mobility needs.

Confirm who supplies the helmet, suitable footwear and any body protector required for the activity. Ask whether closed shoes and long trousers are mandatory, whether fitting time is included and whether the venue provides secure storage. Do not infer safety equipment from competition photographs or promotional images.

For a child, ask whether a guardian must remain on site, whether the adult may accompany the activity, and where handover occurs. For a competition visit, follow the event’s accreditation, veterinary and stable-access rules. The FEI schedules document controlled competition environments; they do not establish public access to stable areas.

FAQ

Which venue is better for a first riding enquiry?

Horseshoe Point is the clearer first contact because its operator page explicitly lists private lessons, group lessons, trail rides and pony rides. That is not a guarantee of a slot or a current price. Send the rider’s age, experience and desired format and wait for confirmation before travelling.

Can visitors watch polo?

Thai Polo publishes named fixtures and event dates, but a past or current-season listing does not establish permanent daily spectator access. Ask about the exact event, ticket or registration requirement, arrival gate, dress expectations and weather changes. General 08:00–17:00 business hours are not an event calendar.

What should I ask before paying?

Request the exact activity, duration, rider eligibility, horse and equipment provision, instruction format, complete price, taxes, deposit, cancellation and weather terms. Add gate and pickup details. For polo, also ask about the experience prerequisite, horse hire, tack, groom support and chukka count.
